# Act I — 20 monster concept sheets

User request: create multiple sessions to generate 20 monster concepts based on Diablo II Act I.

## Deliverable per session
One actual AI-generated PNG concept sheet for one original monster derived from its assigned D2 family. 16:9 sheet: top row front, side, rear and elevated game 3/4 views; bottom row attack pose, black silhouette and small game-scale view. Same anatomy/equipment in every view. Flat dark neutral grey #3A3A3A background, no scene, no text, numbers, watermark or logo. Small neutral adult human scale silhouette at left (1 H = 1.8 m); assigned monster H is an art-direction target, not canonical D2 data.

## Style
Match recent project asset sheets, semi-realistic low detail, broad forms and 2–3 value masses, cold desaturated Act I palette, dirty ivory bones, rusty iron, old wool, muddy worn leather. Upper-left soft neutral-cool lighting; readable forms, no bloom/rim lighting or all-black unreadable characters. European gothic countryside descending into corrupted monastery. No cartoon or shiny modern armor. Restrained horror, no gratuitous gore. Original designs inspired by source families; no copied sprites or Blizzard logos.

Read art-direction/00_TOM_TAT_ART_DIRECTION.md, 06_NHAN_VAT_VA_QUAI.md, 12_QUY_CHUAN_QUAI_CANH_QUAN_CONG_TRINH.md and art-output/mmo-hub-bloodmoor/assets_2d/BRIEF_NATURE_PROPS.md for continuity. ## Generation and handoff
Use imagegen skill and actual built-in image generation. One requested monster = one sheet. Check actual image and save final to your assigned folder/concept.png; record actual prompt, generation method and QA in your assigned folder/README.md. Do not substitute procedural/code drawing, copied web art, SVG or placeholder images. If image generation is unavailable in your session, report BLOCKED clearly; do not claim images exist, do not use CLI/API fallback without explicit user authorization, and do not spawn additional sessions. Do not modify unrelated files, commit or push. Retry at most twice for clear visual defects.
