## Q front_back
POSE = NEUTRAL STANDING (the most important requirement). This creature is a four-legged animal: there is NO T-pose and nothing is held out. It stands square, calm and symmetric on all four legs, like the rest pose of a skeleton rig. Proportions as in the concept: {{PROPORTIONS}}
- LEGS: all four paws flat on one ground line; the two front legs vertical and parallel under the shoulders with a clear gap of empty background between them; the two hind legs in the same relaxed standing posture, also parallel with a clear gap between them; the stance about as wide as the chest. Paws point straight forward: never splayed outward, never braced forward, never crouched, never lunging, no leg lifted, no leg crossed.
- BODY: spine straight and level, no sway and no hunch, no tilt; the left and right halves are mirror images in stance. The belly is clearly above the ground; only the four paws touch it.
- HEAD: carried in the concept's natural carriage (forward and heavy, not raised and not lowered further), pointing straight at the camera in the FRONT view and straight away from it in the BACK view; never turned, tilted, sniffing or snarling; mouth closed; ears hanging in their resting position.
- TAIL: hangs down and slightly back as one simple tapered shape, so that it is seen in the BACK view; never curled, never raised, never tucked between the legs.
- Thin or loose parts (hair tufts, whiskers, chain links, the tail tip) become chunky simple shapes that hug the body; nothing hangs free in a thin line.
- The FRONT and BACK views have the same height, the same body width and the same floor line. In the FRONT view the head is not enlarged by perspective; in the BACK view the rump is not enlarged.
- CROSS-VIEW: parts that wrap around the body (collar, belt, strap, chain) and the tail follow the per-view list below exactly: whatever a nearer body mass hides in a view stays hidden, and every height is the same in every view.

## A front_back
POSE = NEUTRAL STANDING (the most important requirement). This creature is a MULTI-LEGGED animal (spider, insect or other many-legged body): there is NO T-pose and nothing is held out. It stands square, calm and symmetric on all its legs, like the rest pose of a skeleton rig, the body level and every leg relaxed and evenly placed. Proportions as in the concept: {{PROPORTIONS}}
- LEGS: every leg tip rests on ONE ground line; no leg lifted, raised, waving or curled under the body. Seen from above the legs radiate evenly around the body like the spokes of a wheel, in mirror pairs: the front pairs reach forward and outward, the rear pairs backward and outward, the left and right leg of each pair at exactly the same angle and height. Each leg keeps the concept's segments and joints: it rises from the body to a knee that stands higher than the body's leg sockets, then goes down to its tip; all knees at about the same height. In the FRONT and BACK views the legs of each side fan out from the body like a hand of cards, each leg a separate bar where possible, the tips spread evenly along the ground out to the outermost tip; no leg crosses another leg, and no leg crosses in front of the face or the body, where this can be avoided.
- OCCLUSION: where two legs overlap in a view, the leg nearer the camera hides the farther one as a solid opaque shape. Every visible leg is fully opaque and has the same colour and value as the others: NO pale, faded, greyed-out, ghosted or semi-transparent legs (a concept sheet that draws the far legs faded uses a drawing convention, not the design).
- LEGS THICK ENOUGH TO BUILD: every leg is a chunky bar as thick as in the concept or thicker, tapering only toward the tip, which stays blunt; never thin as a wire or a needle; joints are simple chunky rings or knobs.
- BODY: level and straight, centred between the legs, no tilt, no rearing, no crouch, no lunge, no lean to one side; the left and right halves are mirror images in stance. The underside of the body is clearly above the ground; only the leg tips touch it.
- HEAD: straight along the body axis, pointing straight at the camera in the FRONT view and straight away from it in the BACK view; never turned, tilted, raised or lowered; mouthparts, fangs and feelers folded close in front of the face as chunky simple shapes, not spread.
- Thin or loose parts (bristles, antennae, silk strands, web, the tips of feelers) become chunky simple shapes that hug the body, or are left out; nothing hangs free in a thin line.
- The FRONT and BACK views have the same height, the same leg-tip span and the same floor line. In the FRONT view the head and front legs are not enlarged by perspective; in the BACK view the rear of the body and the rear legs are not enlarged.
- CROSS-VIEW: parts that wrap around the body (bands, straps, wraps) and the number and placement of the legs follow the per-view list below exactly: whatever a nearer body mass or a nearer leg hides in a view stays hidden, and every height is the same in every view.

## W front_back
POSE = NEUTRAL WINGED DISPLAY (the most important requirement). This creature is a WINGED animal (bird, moth or other winged insect): there is NO T-pose and nothing is held. It stands calm and symmetric on its legs with both wings fully spread out to the sides in ONE UPRIGHT PLANE, like a heraldic displayed eagle or a cormorant drying its wings: the rest pose of a skeleton rig, in which the wings take the place of the arms of a T-pose. Proportions as in the concept: {{PROPORTIONS}}
- WINGS: both wings fully opened and spread out to the left and right of the body, mirror images of each other, each wing one open flat surface (not folded, not cupped, not curved forward or back, not flapping, not drooping down to the ground like a cape). Both wings lie in one upright plane across the body, just behind the shoulders and square to the camera, so that the FRONT view shows the whole underside of both wings face-on and the BACK view the whole upper side face-on; the wings are not swept back and are not seen edge-on in these two views. The leading edge of each wing (its thick front edge) runs out from the shoulder in one straight line rising about 15 to 20 degrees above horizontal (or at the angle the proportions give), and the wing surface hangs below it. Every wing stays clear of the legs and of the ground, with a gap of empty background between the lowest wing edge and the legs.
- WING BUILD: fore- and hindwings (insects) keep the concept's shapes and overlap as in the concept; feathers (birds) are a few large chunky feather groups with clean separated blunt tips, never single thin feathers. A wing is a solid shape with some thickness and a thick leading edge: membranes are OPAQUE painted surfaces in the concept's colours (a translucent or smoky concept wing becomes an opaque wing of that colour, with its veins painted as broad lines), never see-through and never thin as paper.
- LEGS: standing; every foot or leg tip rests on ONE ground line, legs in mirror pairs at the same angle and height, no leg lifted, tucked or crossed; a bird stands on both legs vertical and parallel under the body with a clear gap of background between them, toes spread flat on the ground and pointing forward; an insect's legs fan evenly out to the sides like the legs of a many-legged creature, every leg a chunky bar, never a wire, every visible leg solid and opaque (no pale or ghost legs).
- BODY: centred between the wings and over the legs, in the concept's natural standing carriage (a bird's back may slope down toward the tail as drawn), mirror-symmetric, no tilt to one side, no lean, no crouch, no take-off. The belly is above the ground.
- HEAD: straight along the body axis, pointing straight at the camera in the FRONT view and straight away from it in the BACK view; never turned or tilted; beak or mouthparts closed; antennae and feelers as chunky simple shapes, symmetric. A proboscis or long beak points straight forward and down along the body axis as one chunky tapered spike.
- TAIL (birds): fanned slightly and pointing straight back, as one simple chunky shape.
- Thin or loose parts (antennae, bristles, loose feathers, fluff, legs of a tiny insect) become chunky simple shapes that hug the body; nothing hangs free in a thin line.
- The FRONT and BACK views have the same height, the same wingspan and the same floor line; no perspective enlargement of the head or the rear.
- CROSS-VIEW: the wings, wing markings (eye-spots, bands), the number of legs and any wrapped or worn parts follow the per-view list below exactly: whatever a nearer body mass or a nearer wing hides in a view stays hidden, and every height is the same in every view.
